# Progression: visible change with rules, cost, and meaning

## More than power levels

Progression can track power, skill, knowledge, mystery, wealth, institution, status, territory, relationship, identity, reputation, or moral capacity. Select tracks that alter choices and embody the reader contract. A number increasing without a changed option space is not narrative progress.

For every track define:

- current reader-visible evidence;
- desired next state;
- acquisition rule;
- cost and opportunity cost;
- counters and failure modes;
- how opposition and environment respond;
- what new choices become possible;
- what old strategy becomes obsolete or dangerous.

## System rule bible

For LitRPG and explicit systems, record terms, units, thresholds, acquisition, spending, cooldowns, stacking, information access, exceptions, and known exploits. The author may discover plot organically, but rules used to resolve plot cannot be improvised invisibly at payoff time.

Introduce a rule through use and consequence before complex exception. Show the character perceiving and operating the system. If the game layer is invisible to the character and never changes decisions, reconsider whether it is LitRPG or merely author-facing metadata.

## Reward gate

A reward should satisfy most of these:

- connected to a prior promise and effort/choice;
- understandable under established rules;
- changes available strategies;
- carries a cost, responsibility, exposure, or counter-response;
- expressed through character meaning, not only statistics;
- creates or sharpens future pressure.

Unexpected rewards may surprise, but their consequence must be legible. Avoid reward showers that force the reader to parse inventory without knowing what matters.

## Tension under growth

Do not preserve tension only by spawning a numerically stronger enemy. Use:

- asymmetric counters and terrain;
- incomplete information;
- social or legal constraints;
- collateral risk and protection duties;
- time, resource, reputation, or moral costs;
- opponents learning from prior tactics;
- incompatible goals;
- failure that permanently changes the path.

Let protagonists lose in consequential ways. A setback is not real if restored immediately without changed options, trust, time, or cost.

## Compress grind, dramatize decisions

Training and resource accumulation deserve scene space when they reveal a rule, require strategy, change a relationship, carry risk, or pay off a prior limitation. Otherwise summarize or skip. Repetition can create satisfaction only when the reader tracks improvement and variation.

## Power-creep audit

At each arc boundary, compare old and new problems:

- Are earlier abilities still useful in transformed ways?
- Did growth erase a promised vulnerability without replacement meaning?
- Does the setting react to demonstrated power?
- Are rule exceptions accumulating to protect outcomes?
- Can a reader predict what evidence would prove success or failure?
- Has spectacle outrun character desire?

Repair by clarifying rules, restoring costs, changing problem dimensions, consolidating abilities, letting opposition adapt, or retiring a track with a meaningful payoff.

## Progression deliverable

Deliver progression tracks, rule ledger, reward schedule, counters, failure states, opposition-response map, information introduction plan, and arc-boundary power-creep audit.
