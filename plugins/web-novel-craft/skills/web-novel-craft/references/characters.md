# Characters and viewpoint

## Three reader connections

Diagnose a major character on three separate axes:

- **empathy**: the reader can understand the character's experience or values;
- **rooting interest**: the reader wants to see what this person attempts and whether it moves;
- **progress**: the reader can perceive a direction of change, discovery, competence, relationship, or self-knowledge.

Likeability, proactivity, and competence are adjustable sliders rather than commandments. An abrasive character can work through legible motive, active pursuit, striking competence, vulnerability, fascination, or a meaningful progress vector. Competence must be demonstrated within a bounded domain; universal effortless mastery removes uncertainty.

## Desire, experience, behavior

For each major character, connect:

```text
formative/current experience -> present desire -> characteristic strategy
-> consequential choice -> cost -> revised desire or strategy
```

Motivation becomes credible through decisions under pressure, not biography alone. Avoid one-note motivation: repeating the same slogan or loved one's name does not deepen desire. Let new evidence, incompatible obligations, and cost reveal different facets.

Distinguish:

- flaw: a belief or habitual behavior that produces damaging choices;
- limitation: an internal or external boundary;
- handicap: a concrete disadvantage in a context;
- wound/history: explanatory experience, not a destiny;
- quirk: a memorable surface behavior that should connect to constraint, choice, relationship, or plot if emphasized.

## Dilemma and cognitive pressure

A dilemma is not merely two painful outcomes. Give the character two obligations, values, identities, or relationships that both have legitimate claims and cannot be fully honored under the current time, resource, information, or authority constraint. The character must own the consequential choice; refusal may remain an option, but it must have its own causal result rather than serving as escape.

Record the character's working model of the situation: what they believe protects people, preserves order, proves worth, or prevents recurrence. Pressure may expose that model as false, incomplete, obsolete, locally useful, or morally insufficient. Do not force every dilemma to share a hidden wrong belief. Reality can contain genuine conflicts between valid duties, and an arc may revise, narrow, harden, or consciously retain a belief rather than destroy it.

Choice may emerge through deliberation, habit, trained reflex, fear, shame, attachment, refusal, or interrupted judgment. Its mode changes the scene, but the action must still reveal a priority for which the character remains accountable. Do not turn every decisive act into involuntary psychology or explain it afterward with a complete diagnostic monologue.

## Strength shadow

Do not assume every virtue is secretly vice. A strength becomes damaging when it is extended beyond its useful domain, used at excessive intensity, or left without a countervalue. Track:

```text
core strength -> prior success -> overextension condition
-> ignored countervalue or blind spot -> real gain + collateral harm
-> recognition, denial, or revised use -> future test
```

Protection without respect for agency can become control; reason without uncertainty or empathy can instrumentalize people. The strength must earn a real result as well as a cost. Do not require it to cause the worst imaginable outcome merely to prove a flaw.

## Proactivity without false control

A protagonist can be overwhelmed yet active if they choose priorities, interpret events, refuse, seek, bargain, hide, protect, investigate, or pay a cost. Action is not only physical aggression. When events keep happening to the protagonist, give their response a strategy that changes the next state.

## Every character owns a story

Write each important character's desire, pressure, strategy, knowledge boundary, and off-page action. The antagonist needs a self-consistent objective and value model, not merely a function of blocking the hero. Supporting characters should not freeze when outside the viewpoint.

Use attention deliberately. A vivid side character can become a “gorilla in a phone booth,” drawing more reader expectation than the planned role can pay. Either reduce emphasis or redesign the outline to honor the attention promise.

## Arc test

An arc is not mandatory improvement. It is a traceable relationship between pressure, choice, cost, and changed belief/strategy/status. Flat and tragic arcs still require meaningful tests. For each turning point, record:

- prior belief or strategy;
- pressure that makes it insufficient;
- available alternatives;
- choice made and price paid;
- evidence of the new state;
- future situation that will test whether the change holds.

## Viewpoint choice

Choose viewpoint for information control and reader experience:

- first person: strong voice and intimacy, tight knowledge boundary, potential reliability play;
- third limited: flexible intimacy with clear filtering and stable information control;
- omniscient: broad thematic and social range, but requires an intentional narrator and transition discipline;
- multiple viewpoints: contrast and scale, but each switch spends orientation and attachment.

Set a switching rule and earn deviations. Introduce a scene through the viewpoint's immediate attention, goal, sensory priorities, and interpretation. Do not leak another character's private thought without an established mode.

## Voice

Voice comes from what the character notices and omits, vocabulary, syntax, rhythm, metaphor sources, social posture, certainty, humor, and evasion. Catchphrases alone produce costume. Test by removing names from a passage; identity should remain inferable without caricature.

## Character deliverable

Deliver character cards, relationship tensions, knowledge boundaries, POV rules, turning-point arcs, voice samples tied to cognition, and a diagnosis of empathy/rooting/progress plus likeability/proactivity/competence. When the task centers on dilemma or consequential cost, also deliver incompatible obligations, working model, choice mode, strength-shadow conditions, price paid, and the later test.
