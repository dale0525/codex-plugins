# Development: choosing a story worth writing for the long run

## Start with two honest intents

Record the author's expressive intent and commercial constraints separately. Expression without a reader model can become private symbolism; market imitation without genuine fascination produces a mismatched voice and exhausts quickly. The goal is not a compromise topic but a story in which the author's durable curiosity creates a repeatable reader experience.

Distinguish an in-story payoff from an author fantasy. “This will become a masterpiece, earn recognition, or make money” is an external hope. A reader can perceive only what happens through the protagonist: a humiliation reversed, a mystery reframed, a relationship changed, a competence demonstrated, a price paid, or a world newly understood.

## Build a reader contract

Write four statements:

1. **Audience**: who reaches for this story, in what reading situation, seeking what appetite.
2. **Recurring experience**: the emotional/intellectual reward the story can deliver repeatedly with variation.
3. **Protagonist engine**: what the protagonist wants now and the characteristic way they act.
4. **Pressure engine**: what keeps generating consequential opposition after one problem is solved.

Test the contract with three hypothetical chapters. If the only reusable move is “a stronger enemy appears” or “another face-slap occurs,” the engine is under-specified. Add relationship, mystery, cost, status, moral, resource, or world-response tracks that alter the meaning of later victories.

## Premise pressure test

Use a causal sentence:

```text
When [inciting change], [specific protagonist] pursues [present desire]
by [characteristic strategy], but [counterforce] makes failure cost [stakes],
forcing choices that test [central value or contradiction].
```

Reject or revise when:

- the desire is a passive condition such as “live peacefully” with no present action;
- the opposition could disappear without changing the story;
- the stakes are generic death or world destruction but not personally legible;
- the protagonist's background does not explain the desire or strategy;
- the premise describes lore rather than a choice-producing conflict;
- the recurring experience cannot vary without violating the contract.

## Series-engine card

Define:

- problem generator;
- protagonist response grammar;
- progression reward;
- consequence that prevents reset;
- renewal rule that makes resolution cause the next pressure;
- escalation dimensions other than raw magnitude;
- exhaustion signal and planned mode change.

Escalation can widen responsibility, deepen intimacy, narrow time, change information, alter legitimacy, reverse alliances, expose cost, or force incompatible promises. Scale is only one dimension.

## Genre contract without formula worship

List the experiences readers reasonably expect from the chosen genre and which expectations will be honored, delayed, transformed, or refused. A subversion still needs the reader to recognize the promise being transformed. Do not import surface furniture from a trend while rejecting the emotional contract that made it work.

Platform packaging is external context, not a substitute for premise and reader experience. Only address current platform rules when the user asks, and verify them from current official sources.

## Development deliverable

Deliver a concept brief, premise, reader contract, series engine, three genuinely different chapter examples, progression candidates, known creative risks, and the next useful writing decision. Do not generate a giant world bible before the story proves which facts create decisions.
